Denise's journey through a flagged claim

Five stages matching ClaimsDock's actual workflow. Built to inform the product, not to stand alone — read alongside the Denise Marsh persona.

Stage 1Queue triage Stage 2Investigating a flagged claim Stage 3Asking Anchor a policy question Stage 4Adjusting a payout Stage 5Closing the loop
Goal Get through her queue without wasting time on claims that only look complicated. Tell a real problem apart from a claim that only looks complicated. Confirm a policy detail without digging through the full plan document herself. Make a decision she can stand behind later, without every action feeling all-or-nothing. Leave a record that holds up if someone questions this decision months from now.
Action Scans the worklist, sorted by severity, to decide what needs her attention first. Opens the Claim Card, reads the evidence and the confidence tier assigned to it. Asks Anchor a specific question about this claim or the policy behind it. Approves, edits, escalates, or denies — including a written justification for a denial. The decision and her reasoning post to the audit log automatically.

Before ClaimsDock Can't tell which claims are safe to skim without reading each one closely. A confidence score alone doesn't say how much weight to put on a finding. Trusts or distrusts a recommendation with no way to actually check its work. Has to accept or reject an automated finding wholesale; a denial's own justification step can become a rubber stamp instead of real oversight. Institutional knowledge lives in individual adjusters' heads and leaves when they do.
After ClaimsDock Status, severity, and SLA are visible at the row level, before she opens anything. A four-tier rubric (High Confidence / Confident / Suspected / Uncertain) states how directly the evidence supports the finding — not just a number. Anchor cites the specific source document behind its answer, not just a confidence label. "Approve with edit" corrects the math without rejecting the whole finding. Deny requires four structured fields, checked for coherence against the claim's actual evidence — not just a click. Every decision's reasoning is logged permanently — a record that outlasts any one adjuster's memory.
If this step fails A genuinely urgent claim gets skimmed past if that row-level data isn't trustworthy. A missed fraud pattern still reaches a human — uncertain findings route to her regardless of category, never auto-approved. A citation sounds right but doesn't actually say what it's cited for — still worth reading the source, not just the answer. A justification check that's too easy to satisfy stops functioning as oversight — why it's checked against the claim's own evidence, not just filled-in text. An audit log nobody reviews is just storage, not oversight — its value depends on someone actually reading it.
